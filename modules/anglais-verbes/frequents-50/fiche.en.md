## The essentials

An irregular verb does not form its past with *-ed*. There are three forms to learn:

1. the **base form**: *go*;
2. the **past simple** (a finished action at a given time): *I **went** to London last year*;
3. the **past participle** (after *have* or *be*): *I have **gone***, *it was **taken***.

> With a past time marker (*yesterday*, *last week*, *in 2020*, *two days ago*), use the past simple, without an auxiliary. After *have*, *has* or *had*, use the past participle.

## Families that help

- **Three different forms, participle in *-en* or *-n***: *take – took – taken*, *give – gave – given*, *write – wrote – written*, *speak – spoke – spoken*, *see – saw – seen*, *know – knew – known*.
- **Same past simple and participle**: *have – had – had*, *make – made – made*, *say – said – said*, *tell – told – told*, *find – found – found*, *keep – kept – kept*, *leave – left – left*, *feel – felt – felt*, *mean – meant – meant*.
- **Ending in *-ought***: *bring – brought – brought*, *think – thought – thought*.
- **Participle same as the base form**: *come – came – come*, *become – became – become*.
- **Compounds follow their verb**: *understand* behaves like *stand*, *become* like *come*.

## Classic traps

- ***I have went*, *I seen*, *I done***: the past simple stands alone (*I went*, *I saw*, *I did*); the past participle needs an auxiliary (*I have gone*, *I have seen*, *I have done*).
- ***was* or *were***: *was* with *I, he, she, it*; *were* with *you, we, they*.
- ***brought* and *bought***: *bring – brought* (*apporter*), *buy – bought* (*acheter*).
- ***found***: past of *find* (*trouver*), but also the base form of the regular verb *to found* (*fonder*).
- ***leave* and *live***: *leave – left – left* (*partir*), *live – lived* (*vivre*, regular).
- **Spelling**: *said* (not *sayed*), *written* (double *t*), *knew* (with a *k*).
- **Pronunciation**: *said* rhymes with *red*, *meant* with *sent*; *read* is spelt the same in all three forms but sounds like *red* in the past.
