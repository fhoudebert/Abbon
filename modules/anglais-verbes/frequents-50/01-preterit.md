## go · single
Quel est le prétérit de *to go* (aller) ?

- [x] went
- [ ] goed
- [ ] gone
- [ ] go

> *go – went – gone*. Le prétérit est **went** : *I went to London last year.* *Gone* est le participe passé (*she has gone*), et *goed* n'existe pas : *to go* ne prend jamais *-ed*.

- [ ] goed

- [ ] go

> *go – went – gone*. The past simple is **went**: *I went to London last year.* *Gone* is the past participle (*she has gone*), and *goed* does not exist: *to go* never takes *-ed*.

## be · single
Complétez : *They ___ at home last night.*

- [x] were
- [ ] was
- [ ] been
- [ ] are

> *To be* est le seul verbe à avoir deux formes au prétérit : **was** avec *I, he, she, it* et **were** avec *you, we, they*. *Been* est le participe passé ; *are* est du présent, alors que *last night* demande un passé.

### en
Complete: *They ___ at home last night.*

- [x] were
- [ ] was
- [ ] been
- [ ] are

> *To be* is the only verb with two past simple forms: **was** with *I, he, she, it* and **were** with *you, we, they*. *Been* is the past participle; *are* is present tense, whereas *last night* calls for a past form.

> *make – made – made*. Le prétérit est **made**. *Maked* n'existe pas. *Did* est le prétérit de *to do* : les deux verbes se traduisent souvent par « faire », mais on dit *make a cake* (fabriquer) et *do the dishes* (accomplir une tâche).

> *make – made – made*. The past simple is **made**. *Maked* does not exist. *Did* is the past simple of *to do*: you *make a cake* (create something) but *do the dishes* (carry out a task).

## know · single
Quel est le prétérit de *to know* (savoir, connaître) ?

- [x] knew
- [ ] knowed
- [ ] known
- [ ] new

> *know – knew – known*. Le prétérit est **knew**, avec un *k* muet comme dans *know*. Il se prononce comme l'adjectif *new* (nouveau), d'où la confusion à l'écrit. *Known* est le participe passé.

### en
What is the past simple of *to know*?

- [x] knew
- [ ] knowed
- [ ] known
- [ ] new

> *know – knew – known*. The past simple is **knew**, with a silent *k* as in *know*. It sounds like the adjective *new*, hence the spelling confusion. *Known* is the past participle.
