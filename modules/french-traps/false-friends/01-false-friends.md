## actuellement · single
Your French colleague says: « Actuellement, je travaille à Lyon. » What does she mean?

- [x] At the moment, she works in Lyon
- [ ] Actually, she works in Lyon (contrary to what you thought)
- [ ] She used to work in Lyon
- [ ] She will soon work in Lyon

> *Actuellement* means **currently, at present**. To say "actually", use **en fait** or **en réalité**: « En fait, je travaille à Lyon. »

## librairie · single
You are looking for a library to borrow a book. Where do you go?

- [x] To the *bibliothèque*: a *librairie* is a bookshop, where you buy books
- [ ] To the *librairie*: it is the French word for library
- [ ] To the *libraire*: it is the building where books are lent
- [ ] To the *médiathèque*, because French libraries do not lend books

> A **libraire** is a bookseller, a **librairie** a bookshop. The library is the **bibliothèque** (or, in many towns, the *médiathèque*, which also lends films and music). Both come from Latin: *liber*, book, and Greek *biblion*.

## eventuellement · single
« Je viendrai éventuellement samedi. » What can you expect?

- [x] Maybe they will come on Saturday, maybe not
- [ ] They will come in the end, on Saturday, after some delay
- [ ] They will definitely come on Saturday
- [ ] They will come every Saturday

> *Éventuellement* means **possibly, if need be**. "Eventually", in the sense of "in the end", is **finalement** or **à la fin**.

## attendre · single
Which sentence means "I attended the meeting"?

- [x] J'ai assisté à la réunion.
- [ ] J'ai attendu la réunion.
- [ ] J'ai aidé à la réunion.
- [ ] J'ai attendu à la réunion.

> **Attendre** means **to wait (for)**: « J'ai attendu le bus », "I waited for the bus" (no preposition in French). **Assister à** means **to attend**, and **aider** means to help (or to assist, in English).

## sensible · single
« Mon frère est très sensible. » How would you translate it?

- [x] My brother is very sensitive.
- [ ] My brother is very sensible.
- [ ] My brother is very sensual.
- [ ] My brother is very serious.

> *Sensible* means **sensitive** (easily hurt, or responsive). A **sensible** person, in English, is **raisonnable** or **sensé(e)** in French.

## preservatif · single
In a French shop, why should you not ask whether a jam contains « des préservatifs » ?

- [x] *Un préservatif* is a condom: preservatives are **des conservateurs**
- [ ] It is a rude word for jam
- [ ] It means "a jar" in Canadian French
- [ ] Shops are not allowed to sell them

> A classic, and embarrassing, false friend. On food labels, look for *sans conservateurs*, "no preservatives".

## location · single
On a French website you see « location de voitures ». What is offered?

- [x] Car rental
- [ ] A map showing where the cars are
- [ ] A car park
- [ ] Second-hand cars for sale

> **Une location** is a **rental** (*louer*, to rent). A location, in the sense of a place, is **un lieu**, **un emplacement** or **un endroit**.

## true-friends · multiple
Which pairs really mean the same thing in French and English?

- [x] un hôpital – a hospital
- [x] la musique – music
- [ ] une journée – a journey
- [ ] la monnaie – money
- [ ] un coin – a coin

> *Une journée* is **a day** (a journey is **un voyage** or **un trajet**). *La monnaie* is **change** or **currency** (money is **l'argent**). *Un coin* is **a corner** (a coin is **une pièce**). Thousands of French and English words are true friends, mostly thanks to the Norman Conquest: do not distrust them all!

## piece · short
How do you say "a coin" in French? Write only the noun, without the article.

= pièce
= piece

> **Une pièce** (de monnaie). The same word also means a room (*une maison de cinq pièces*), a play (*une pièce de théâtre*) and a piece (*une pièce du puzzle*).

## plein · single
At the end of a big meal, a French host asks if you want more. Why should you avoid « Non merci, je suis plein » ?

- [x] *Plein* sounds as if you were drunk, and *pleine* is used for a pregnant animal: say « Non merci, je n'ai plus faim » or « J'ai très bien mangé »
- [ ] It is grammatically wrong: *plein* cannot follow *être*
- [ ] It means "I am bored"
- [ ] It is only used for cars: "the tank is full"

> *Être plein* is slang for being drunk, and *une vache pleine* is a cow in calf. The polite options compliment the meal: « C'était délicieux, je n'ai plus faim. »

## excite · single
How do you say "I'm so excited to see you!" in French?

- [x] J'ai tellement hâte de te voir !
- [ ] Je suis tellement excité de te voir !
- [ ] Je suis tellement exalté de te voir !
- [ ] J'ai tellement envie d'exciter de te voir !

> *Être excité(e)* often has a sexual or agitated connotation. Use **avoir hâte de** (to look forward to), **être impatient(e)** or **être ravi(e)**. *Excitant* is fine for a project or a film, but the safest is *passionnant*.

## presenter · single
How do you say "Let me introduce my sister"?

- [x] Je te présente ma sœur.
- [ ] Je t'introduis ma sœur.
- [ ] Laisse-moi introduire ma sœur.
- [ ] Je te montre ma sœur.

> **Présenter** is used for people. **Introduire** means to insert, or to bring something in (*introduire une clé dans la serrure*, *introduire une loi*): "introducing" your sister that way would sound very odd.
