## avoir · cloze
Complete with the right verb.

= J'{ai ~suis} faim, j'{ai ~suis} froid et j'{ai ~suis} vingt-cinq ans.

> French uses **avoir** where English uses "to be": *avoir faim* (to be hungry), *avoir soif*, *avoir froid*, *avoir chaud*, *avoir peur*, *avoir raison*, and the age: *j'ai 25 ans*, literally "I have 25 years".

## depuis · single
How do you say "I have been living in Paris for two years" (and I still do)?

- [x] J'habite à Paris depuis deux ans.
- [ ] J'ai habité à Paris depuis deux ans.
- [ ] J'ai habité à Paris pour deux ans.
- [ ] Je suis habité à Paris depuis deux ans.

> For an action that started in the past and **is still going on**, French uses the **present** with **depuis**. *J'ai habité à Paris pendant deux ans* means you lived there for two years and no longer do.

## countries · cloze
Complete with the right preposition.

= Cet été, je vais {en ~au ~à} France, {au ~en ~à} Japon, {aux ~en ~au} États-Unis et {à ~en ~au} Rome.

> **en** before feminine countries (most end in *-e*: *la France*), **au** before masculine ones (*le Japon*, *le Canada*), **aux** before plural ones (*les États-Unis*), and **à** before cities.

## gender · multiple
Which of these nouns are feminine?

- [x] plage (beach)
- [x] mer (sea)
- [x] voiture (car)
- [ ] problème (problem)
- [ ] musée (museum)

> *La plage, la mer, la voiture*, but *le problème, le musée* (and *le silence*). The ending *-e* is not a reliable rule; nouns ending in *-ème* and *-ée* are often masculine (*le système, le lycée*). Learn every noun with its article.

## cest-il-est · single
Which sentence is correct?

- [x] Il est médecin. / C'est un médecin.
- [ ] Il est un médecin. / C'est médecin.
- [ ] C'est médecin. / Il est un médecin.
- [ ] Il est un médecin. / Il est médecin.

> With a profession, nationality or religion and **no article**, use **il est / elle est**: *Elle est française*. With an article or a determiner, use **c'est**: *C'est une Française*, *C'est mon médecin*.

## etre-avoir · single
Which sentence is correct?

- [x] Hier, je suis allé au cinéma.
- [ ] Hier, j'ai allé au cinéma.
- [ ] Hier, je suis allé à le cinéma.
- [ ] Hier, j'étais allé au cinéma.

> About fifteen verbs of movement or change of state take **être** in the *passé composé*: *aller, venir, arriver, partir, entrer, sortir, monter, descendre, naître, mourir, tomber, rester…* (and the reflexive verbs). And **à + le** always becomes **au**.

## ancien · single
« Mon ancien professeur » and « une maison ancienne »: what is the difference?

- [x] Before the noun, *ancien* means **former**; after it, **old, antique**
- [ ] There is none: the position of adjectives is free
- [ ] Before the noun, it means **very old**; after it, **a little old**
- [ ] *Ancien* can only be placed before the noun

> A few adjectives change meaning with their position: *un grand homme* (a great man) / *un homme grand* (a tall man), *ma propre chambre* (my own room) / *ma chambre propre* (my clean room), *un pauvre homme* (a pitiable man) / *un homme pauvre* (a poor man).

## tu-vous · single
You meet your new manager in a French company for the first time. How do you address her?

- [x] With *vous*, until she suggests *tu*
- [ ] With *tu*: French companies are informal
- [ ] With *tu* if she is younger than you
- [ ] With her first name and *tu*

> *Vous* is the default with strangers, older people and in professional settings; *tu* is for friends, family, children and close colleagues. It is the person of higher status who suggests switching: « On peut se tutoyer ? »

## froid · single
How do you say "It's cold today"?

- [x] Il fait froid aujourd'hui.
- [ ] Il est froid aujourd'hui.
- [ ] C'est froid aujourd'hui.
- [ ] J'ai froid aujourd'hui.

> Weather uses **il fait**: *il fait froid, il fait beau, il fait 30 degrés*. *Il est froid* describes a thing or a person (*le café est froid*), and *j'ai froid* means that **you** feel cold.

## nombre · numeric
In France, which number is « quatre-vingt-dix-sept »?

= 97

> Literally "four twenty ten seven": 4 × 20 + 10 + 7. In Belgium and Switzerland, people say *septante*, *nonante* (and *huitante* in some Swiss cantons), which is much simpler.

## merci · single
Your host asks: « Encore un peu de café ? » You answer « Merci ! » with a little hand gesture. What will your host understand?

- [x] No, thank you
- [ ] Yes, please
- [ ] You are not sure
- [ ] You would like tea instead

> On its own, *merci* in answer to an offer usually means **no, thank you**. To accept, say **« Oui, merci »** or **« Avec plaisir »**. A classic misunderstanding: you may stay thirsty!

## bonsoir · flashcard
What is the difference between « Bonsoir » and « Bonne soirée » ?

> **Bonsoir** is a greeting when you **arrive** in the evening ("good evening"). **Bonne soirée** is said when you **leave**: "have a nice evening". The same goes for *bonjour* / *bonne journée*.
