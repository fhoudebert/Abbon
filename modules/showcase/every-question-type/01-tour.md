## single · single
**Single choice.** Which planet is the largest in the Solar System?

- [x] Jupiter
- [ ] Saturn
- [ ] Neptune
- [ ] Earth

> Jupiter is more than twice as massive as all the other planets combined. In practice mode, you see the explanation right after answering; in exam mode, at the end.

## multiple · multiple
**Multiple choice.** Which of these elements are noble gases?

- [x] Helium ($\ce{He}$)
- [x] Neon ($\ce{Ne}$)
- [x] Argon ($\ce{Ar}$)
- [ ] Nitrogen ($\ce{N2}$)
- [ ] Hydrogen ($\ce{H2}$)

> Noble gases fill the last column of the periodic table: helium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon, radon. Their full outer shell makes them very unreactive. With partial scoring, each correct box earns part of the points.

## short · short · case-sensitive
**Short answer**, case-sensitive. What is the chemical symbol of sodium?

= Na

> From its Latin name, *natrium*. Potassium is K (*kalium*), and iron Fe (*ferrum*). Here capital letters count: *NA* or *na* would be wrong.

## numeric · numeric
**Number with unit.** A train covers 120 km in 1 h 30 min. What is its average speed, in km/h?

= 80 ± 0.5 km/h

> $v = \dfrac{d}{t} = \dfrac{120\ \text{km}}{1.5\ \text{h}} = 80\ \text{km/h}$. Typed numbers accept a comma or a point, and a small tolerance.

## molar-mass · numeric
What is the molar mass of water, $\ce{H2O}$, in g/mol? ($\ce{H} = 1$, $\ce{O} = 16$)

= 18 ± 0.1 g/mol

> $M = 2 \times 1 + 16 = 18\ \text{g/mol}$. One mole of water, 18 g, is about a tablespoon and a half.

## balanced · single
**Chemical equations.** Which equation is balanced?

- [x] $\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}$
- [ ] $\ce{H2 + O2 -> H2O}$
- [ ] $\ce{H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}$
- [ ] $\ce{2H2 + 2O2 -> 2H2O}$

> On each side: 4 hydrogen atoms and 2 oxygen atoms. Abbon draws chemical formulas, equations and units with the mhchem notation.

## combustion · cloze
**Fill-in-the-gaps with drop-down lists.** Balance the combustion of methane.

= CH₄ + {2 ~1 ~3} O₂ → CO₂ + {2 ~1 ~4} H₂O

> $\ce{CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O}$: 1 carbon, 4 hydrogen and 4 oxygen atoms on each side. Each gap counts separately with partial scoring.

## capitals · cloze
**Fill-in-the-gaps, typed.** Complete with the capital cities.

= The capital of Australia is {Canberra}, and the capital of Canada is {Ottawa}.

> Neither Sydney nor Toronto! Canberra was built as a compromise between Sydney and Melbourne, and Ottawa was chosen by Queen Victoria in 1857.

## derivative · expression
**Mathematical expression.** Type the derivative of $f(x) = x^3 - 2x$. Any equivalent form is accepted; on a touch screen, a maths keyboard appears.

= $3x^2 - 2$

> Differentiate term by term: $(x^3)' = 3x^2$ and $(2x)' = 2$. Abbon checks the formula itself, not its spelling: $-2 + 3x^2$ is just as correct.

## expand · expression
Expand $(x + 1)^2$.

= $x^2 + 2x + 1$

> $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$. Careful: $x^2 + 1$ is the classic mistake. (Abbon compares values, so it would also accept the unexpanded form: the instruction is up to you.)

## circle · expression
Write the area of a disc of radius $r$.

= $\pi r^2$

> $A = \pi r^2$; its perimeter is $2\pi r$. Type `\pi` or use the $\pi$ key of the maths keyboard.

## antiderivative · expression
An antiderivative of $2x$? (A constant can be added.)

= $x^2 + C$

> With "+ C", Abbon accepts any antiderivative: $x^2$, $x^2 + 5$ or $x^2 + C$.

## pythagoras · flashcard
**Flashcard.** State Pythagoras' theorem.

> In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides: $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. Turn the card over, then say whether you knew it: flashcards you missed can go to the review box.

## review-box · single
**The review box.** What happens to a question you add to the review box?

- [x] It comes back at increasing intervals (Leitner method) until you know it for good
- [ ] It is deleted from the quiz
- [ ] It is sent to your teacher
- [ ] It comes back every day, forever

> Answer right and the card moves up a box, and comes back later; answer wrong and it goes back to the first box. Points and judo belts reward your regularity.
