## regret-to · single
*We regret ___ you that your application has been unsuccessful.*

- [x] to inform
- [ ] informing
- [ ] inform
- [ ] to informing

> *Regret to do* : regretter de devoir faire, maintenant (annonce d'une mauvaise nouvelle, très formel). *Regret doing* : regretter d'avoir fait (*I regret leaving school early*).

## have-done · single
*She had her car ___ last week.*

- [x] repaired
- [ ] repair
- [ ] to repair
- [ ] repairing

> *Have / get something done* : faire faire par quelqu'un d'autre (forme causative). *She repaired her car* voudrait dire qu'elle l'a réparée elle-même.

## said-to-have · single
*He is said ___ a fortune in the 1990s.*

- [x] to have made
- [ ] to make
- [ ] having made
- [ ] that he made

> Passif impersonnel : *he is said / believed / thought to* + infinitif. Pour un fait antérieur, infinitif passé (*to have made*). Équivalent : *It is said that he made a fortune.*

## believed-to-be · single
*The minister is believed ___ on a new law at the moment.*

- [x] to be working
- [ ] to work
- [ ] working
- [ ] to have worked

> Même structure, avec une action en cours : infinitif progressif (*to be working*). Ces tournures sont fréquentes dans la presse et dans la partie « Use of English » de Cambridge.

## had-better · single
*You'd better ___ now, or you'll miss the last bus.*

- [x] leave
- [ ] to leave
- [ ] leaving
- [ ] left

> *Had better* (« tu ferais mieux de ») + base verbale, sans *to*, même si *had* ressemble à un passé. Négation : *you'd better not leave*.

## cant-help · single
*I can't help ___ about what she said.*

- [x] thinking
- [ ] to think
- [ ] think
- [ ] thought

> *Can't help + -ing* : « ne pas pouvoir s'empêcher de ». Aussi : *can't stand, can't bear* (ne pas supporter) + *-ing*.

## object-to · single
*She strongly objected to ___ like a child.*

- [x] being treated
- [ ] be treated
- [ ] treat
- [ ] treating

> *Object to*, comme *look forward to* et *be used to* : *to* est une préposition, donc gérondif, ici passif (*being treated*).

## neednt-have · single
*You ___ brought any food; there was plenty for everyone.*

- [x] needn't have
- [ ] mustn't have
- [ ] didn't need
- [ ] couldn't have

> *Needn't have* + participe passé : on l'a fait, et c'était inutile. *Didn't need to bring* dit seulement que ce n'était pas nécessaire (sans préciser si on l'a fait).

## would-sooner · single
*I'd sooner ___ at home tonight; I'm exhausted.*

- [x] stay
- [ ] to stay
- [ ] staying
- [ ] stayed

> *Would sooner*, comme *would rather*, + base verbale quand le sujet est le même. Avec un autre sujet : *I'd sooner you stayed.*

## worth-ing · single
*The museum is well worth ___ if you have time.*

- [x] visiting
- [ ] to visit
- [ ] visit
- [ ] being visited

> *Be worth + -ing* : « valoir la peine d'être fait ». Bien que le sens soit passif, la forme reste active : *worth visiting*, et non *worth being visited*.
